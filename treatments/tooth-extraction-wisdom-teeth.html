ORAL SURGERY

Tooth Extraction & Wisdom Tooth Removal in Secunderabad

Gentle removal of damaged, loose or impacted teeth, including surgical wisdom tooth removal, with a plan to replace the tooth if you need one.

When a tooth needs to come out

We always try to save a natural tooth first. Sometimes, though, removing a tooth is the healthiest choice:

  • A tooth broken or decayed too badly to repair
  • A tooth loosened by advanced gum disease
  • An infection that cannot be treated with a root canal
  • Crowding, when braces need space
  • A baby tooth that has not fallen out and is blocking the adult tooth
  • An impacted or problem wisdom tooth

Impacted wisdom teeth

Wisdom teeth (third molars) are the last teeth to come through, usually between the late teens and mid-twenties. Often there is not enough room, and the tooth stays stuck, or impacted, in the jaw or gum. An impacted wisdom tooth can cause:

  • Pain and swelling at the back of the mouth
  • Repeated gum infections around the tooth (pericoronitis)
  • Food trapping and decay in the tooth next to it
  • Difficulty opening the mouth fully

Not every wisdom tooth needs to be removed. If yours is healthy and causing no trouble, the dentist may simply keep an eye on it. When removal is needed, 3D CBCT imaging shows exactly where the tooth sits in relation to the nerve and nearby teeth, so the surgery can be planned carefully.

Simple and surgical extractions

A tooth that is fully visible can usually be removed in a simple extraction. A tooth that is broken at the gum line or impacted needs a surgical extraction, where a small opening is made in the gum and the tooth is removed, sometimes in sections. Both are done under local anaesthesia, by a dentist trained in oral surgery.

Looking after yourself afterwards

  • First 24 hours: Bite on the gauze as advised, rest, and avoid rinsing, spitting, using a straw or smoking so the blood clot can form.
  • Eating: Choose soft, cool foods at first and chew on the other side.
  • From the next day: Rinse gently with warm salt water after meals and keep brushing your other teeth.
  • Call us if: Pain gets worse after two or three days, or there is bleeding or swelling that will not settle.

Replacing a missing tooth

If the tooth that was removed leaves a gap, it can be replaced with a dental implant, a bridge or a denture. Planning tooth replacement is Dr. Anam's own specialty.
